Interactive Hardware Simulation

Experience the Digital Sand Hourglass

Try the real-time falling sand cellular automaton in your browser. This simulation recreates the physics running on dual 8×8 RGB LED matrices controlled by an Arduino and MPU6050 accelerometer.

Physics-Driven Sand on Dual 8×8 LED Displays

Rather than a static countdown, this embedded project models each grain of sand as a discrete physical cell. Grains slide diagonally, form authentic heap pyramids, and tumble through the central bottleneck.

  • Dual 8×8 RGB Matrix (128 total LED nodes)
  • MPU6050 I2C 6-DOF gravity & orientation sensing
  • Auto-flip sensor restart with customizable duration

Background & Education

Beyond the Screen

I'm Sagor Sharif, a Computer Science & Engineering undergraduate at the University of Global Village (UGV) in Barishal, Bangladesh. My work lives where software and hardware collide.

While many software developers stay strictly in the browser, I'm drawn to projects where code breathes life into physical devices: an Arduino-powered safety device transmitting GPS coordinates via GSM, an autonomous rover tracking paths with IR sensors, or digital sand trickling through LED matrices according to gravity.

When I'm not breadboarding circuits or deploying full-stack web applications with Next.js and Supabase, I'm competing in programming contests or mentoring youngsters in STEM workshops. I believe engineering is at its best when it solves real human problems.
